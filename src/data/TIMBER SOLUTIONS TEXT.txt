How to use this master
Each scene is standalone and copy-ready. The image-generation prompt does not ask the image model to render typography; apply the title and supporting line as responsive website overlays during production.
Scene ID  H-01 to H-15 are panoramic subcategory heroes. Close-up IDs follow the subcategory numbers, for example 1a-01 to 1a-09.
Visual format  Heroes use 21:9 panoramic framing. All 156 close-ups use 16:9. Exterior close-ups are closer architectural views; Interior close-ups use the 16-color TRIANGLE WOOD palette and coordinated flooring.
Project-type alternation  Exterior close-up series follow Residential ? Commercial ? Hospitality three times. Interior close-ups follow the palette order while their project types continue alternating Residential ? Commercial ? Hospitality.
Title + hero text  Every scene has a unique lifestyle-led title and one short line of accompanying website copy.
Material description  Each entry uses no more than two concise sentences, retaining the wood character and the key application-specific technical considerations, finish and verification notes.
No-repeat principle  Every close-up has its own location and spatial concept. Architectural details, use cases, camera focus, and subtle life cues are varied deliberately.
Sustainability language  The imagery may suggest traceable sourcing, repairability, low-impact finishes and efficient material use. Do not publish FSC or other certification claims unless TRIANGLE WOOD has documentation for the actual supply chain.
Material naming and technical guardrails
* Iroko: use the common name Iroko, commonly identified with Milicia excelsa and/or Milicia regia. It is sometimes sold as "African teak," but it is not botanical teak.
* Cumaru: Dipteryx odorata, often marketed as "Brazilian teak" despite being a different species from true teak. It is very dense and hard, commonly reddish- to dark brown, and has interlocked grain; predrilling and movement allowances belong in the technical specification.
* Panama Teak: interpreted here as plantation-grown Tectona grandis grown in Panama. Confirm species identity, plantation/source paperwork, grade, moisture content, treatment and supplier technical data before publishing performance or sustainability claims.
* All structural elements - including bridges, pergolas, elevated decking, staircases, handrails and timber houses - require project-specific design and appropriate verification. The scene copy describes visual direction and specification considerations; it does not certify structural suitability.
* No generic claim of being "environmentally friendly," carbon neutral, certified or maintenance-free should be published solely from an image brief. Ask procurement for verified origin, chain-of-custody evidence, finishes and supplier data.
